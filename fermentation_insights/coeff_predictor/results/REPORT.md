### v1 -> v2 (round 4: training protocol, recovery inputs, structures)

1. **Training protocol** (`PROTOCOL`): every network fit -- HPO, all CV schemes, `final_dev` and `release` -- trains on all its samples with sample-level early stopping (each member on its own random 15 % hold-out), without a calibration hold-out and without refit, so the CV estimates the training procedure of the final models with the selected hyperparameters (the HPO is not nested in the CV). v1 held out 15 % of the configurations for calibration and early-stopped every member on about five held-out configurations: noisy and often far too short (dev proxy of the frozen test: best epochs 6-226, in 4 of 6 draws a member stopped at <= 20 epochs). On that proxy the v1 protocol gave OOD 12.0 ± 6.9 % / ID 13.9 ± 5.5 %, the new one 7.2 ± 1.2 % / 3.9 ± 0.2 % (6 draws, paired t = -1.8 / -4.6; the draws share configurations: 24 picks, 16 distinct). Refitting after early stopping did not help (+0.2 pp at twice the cost) and is not used.
2. **Intervals**: the final and release ensembles have no calibration hold-out; their split-conformal quantiles come from the out-of-fold scores of the development leave-one-configuration-out CV of the same procedure and ensemble size (`train.calibrate_conformal_oof`, re-scored with the final ensemble's spread floors, `coverage_guaranteed = False`), with a cross-fitted coverage estimate. v1 calibrated `final_dev` on its own 6 calibration configurations and transferred the release intervals from the dev model scored on its calibration configurations AND the test configurations; v2 never uses test samples for calibration.
3. **Hard productivity structure** (`ModelConfig.r_structure='physics'`): the coefficients of the fermentation capital and of the electricity intensity are theta0(x) + theta1(x) r_ref/r with theta1 >= 0, so dMPSP/dr <= 0 holds by construction (v1 violated it for 0.8-8 % of held-out samples). In the data only the fermentation capital depends materially on r, as 1/r (beta = 1.0, R² = 0.9999 within configurations). Dev productivity CV with sample-level hold-outs: 5.16 -> 4.29 % (seed t = -4.5; low-r side 6.34 -> 4.39 %, seed t = -12.5; per configuration of the single productivity fold 28 of 36 better, t = -2.9); LOCO and random CV unchanged.
4. **Recovery inputs** (masking preset 'physics_v2'): the recovery g no longer sees the reagent (base / acid) prices, which set what the reagents cost, not how much product is recovered (dg/dprice = 0 exactly; within-configuration correlation -0.05 / -0.02). Adopted as a hard physical constraint, not for accuracy: dev CV in-family g unchanged, LOPO -10.5 points (seed t = -1.2; g error seed t = -2.9; per held-out product 3 better / 4 worse, median +0.1), LOFO configuration-balanced -2.5 points (seed t = -2.4; per held-out feedstock 2 better / 2 worse, median -1.0, t over folds -0.7), LOCO neutral. Feedstock, fermentation, scale or titer-domain inputs for g, and a larger g loss weight, did not help and are not used.
5. **Extrapolation safeguards** (`EXTRAPOLATION_SAFEGUARDS`): a sugar-availability prior of the feedstock intensity (free sugars and starch fully available, structural polysaccharides by the saccharification yield; the network predicts only the residual), clipping of the feedstock and pretreatment inputs to their training range, and a neutralization gate (no neutralization cost without an acid group or neutralizing agent; verified on the data). v1 extrapolated a new feedstock's minor composition fractions (corn: 9.5 training sd) into a saturated feedstock efficiency (corn 1/eta 1.50 vs 0.93-1.01). Dev LOFO fold mean 63.4 -> 34.4 % (seed t = -5.3, 5 seeds; corn 128 -> 43 %); over the 4 held-out feedstocks 3 better / 1 worse, median -15.8, t over folds -1.5, corn 72 % of the gain: not established at the fold level, but the corrected feedstock channel is (DESIGN.md 4d: feedstock-channel error 23 -> 3.5 % of the numerator). LOCO and random not worse.
6. **Plan**: the CV, final_dev and release ensembles have the same number of members (the scores divide by the member spread); the PI-NN and the plain MLP run on every LOCO fold, the PI-NN ablations on a stratified subset of configurations (`--ablation-loco-folds`). The hyperparameter search was re-run under the new protocol.

### v2 -> v3 (data corrections and round 5)

**Data** (defects found by the final review of v2; DESIGN.md section 1, "Data corrections" and "Known data defects"). The corrected rows replace the defective ones in training and in the test split: 1550 -> 1600 samples (TAL 185 -> 232, succinic 141 -> 144), development set 1306 -> 1353.

1. **D15, succinic acid at neutral pH** (4 configurations, 72 samples): succinic adapter v2 set only the neutralization flag, and the reactor kept the purchased lime out of the broth, so the salt formation, the acidulation (R401) and the gypsum filtration (S405) were never simulated: the v2 rows equalled the low-pH process except the base cost, while the downstream specification (the model inputs) described acidulation and gypsum filtration. Regenerated with adapter v3 (72 / 72 samples, no failed point): MPSP +0.22 USD/kg vs. the low-pH process at identical TY points and parameters (+0.10 vs. the v2 rows): lime 1 mol/mol succinic acid, 0.89-0.90 kg H2SO4 (93 wt%) and 1.44-1.45 kg wet gypsum per kg; recovery unchanged. Rows of adapter < 3 are rejected (`unpatchable_D15`).
2. **H1, TAL_SA_glucose / TAL_SA_sugarcane**: after a convergence failure whose simulator reset also failed, the old harness wrote the later samples as complete without a simulated point (46 samples, and one cut after 3 points); the quality filter rejected them, so v2 trained on 1 and 10 of 29 samples. Cause: the IPA evaporator F407 (a root search that evaporates all the IPA in one effect); the TAL adapter now returns the exact overall evaporation fraction in that region (re-simulated recorded points change by < 1e-4 in MPSP), the harness reloads after a failed reset, and the 47 samples were re-run (29 / 29 each).
3. **D8, TAL yield basis**: analysed, y kept (the module's yield on all substrates, glucose-eq. by theoretical yield). Its consequence, a feedstock-channel decomposition with a negative titer term from the spiked acetate's sugar substitution (c' < 0 in every TAL sample; no input describes the acetate), is handled by model change 3.

**Model** (round 5; DESIGN.md section 6, round 5: designed on a frozen copy of the v2 tables, development rows only, one-member fits paired over 3 seeds; re-checked on the corrected development rows, table below). Each has a CLI flag that restores v2 (all together `--v2-model`):

1. **Channel loss in surface space with a fixed weight** (`LossConfig.ch_space='surface'`, `w_ch = 3`, no longer an HPO dimension): v2 compared each channel's four coefficients in units of their cross-configuration spread, at the HPO's weight 0.1, which left the decomposition free once MPSP fit (in-sample: every corn configuration's feedstock sensitivity 19-27 % low, the isobutanol electricity response off by 94 % of MPSP). The surface term compares every channel numerator (price channels as price x intensity) at the sample's simulated TY points relative to the total numerator, which is the out-of-fold fidelity metric of section 5. `w_ch` was chosen on out-of-fold fidelity (1 < 3 ~ 10 on LOCO; 3 best on LOFO and LOPO), not by the MPSP criterion, which cannot see the decomposition.
2. **Power gate** (`ModelConfig.power_gate`): where electricity is not among the coproducts (no turbogenerator: corn ethanol, the lactic configurations), every coefficient of the net electricity intensity is mapped to s softplus(q / s) >= 0, so such a plant cannot be predicted to export power (price linearity and dMPSP/dr <= 0 kept). A hard physical constraint, checked on the data (the net electricity is never negative at the 3667 points of those configurations; the gate switches itself off if the data violate it). It removes a qualitative failure (out of fold, v2 predicted export for 85-100 % of the held-out samples of those configurations), at a cost for corn ethanol: its v2 prediction offset a missed DDGS / corn-oil credit with a wrong-sign electricity credit, which the gate removes (open issue).
3. **Known defective channel targets masked in the loss** (`experiments.DEFECTIVE_TARGETS`; this run: feedstock: TAL): the TAL feedstock channel (D8) carries the acetate's sugar substitution, which no input describes and the product-blind feedstock head cannot represent; its totals, g and other channels keep their targets. A correction of defective targets, neutral on the development CV. The study also proposed masking lactic acid's feedstock targets (a recovery shape, not a defect); on the corrected data that helped only the corn leave-one-feedstock-out fold in 2 of 3 seeds and cost leave-one-product-out about 2 points, so it is not used.

## 3. Training protocol

Every network fit of this report -- HPO, every CV scheme (PI-NN, plain MLP, ablations), `models/final_dev` and `models/release` -- uses the same procedure (`experiments.PROTOCOL`): no calibration hold-out; each ensemble member early-stops on its own random 15 % sample-level hold-out (the hold-outs differ between members, so the ensemble sees every sample; patience 40, at least 20 and at most 300 epochs); no refit. The baselines fit exactly the rows the members train on. So the CV numbers estimate the training procedure that made the final models, with the selected hyperparameters: the HPO (section 4) chose them on the same development set and is not nested in the CV, so the CV is slightly optimistic for the whole procedure (HPO + fit). Intervals: section 6.
